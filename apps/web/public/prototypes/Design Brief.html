00Push-back

Five things I want to challenge before we design.

You asked for opinion. Here are the places where I'd consciously diverge from the brief — each with the trade-off I considered and the reason I'd still push.

P1 · Five inbox columns is too many

The brief specifies five columns: New, Urgent, Waiting Staff, Waiting Owner, Resolved. On a 13″ reception laptop at 1440×900 with a sidebar, that's ~220px per column — tighter than Front, tighter than Linear's triage. Owner-card + pet-name + last-message preview + AI tags don't fit at that width.

I'd argue for a single, dense, sortable list with status as a leading column and saved “views” as the primary navigation. Linear and Height ship this. Five Trello-style columns visualise the lifecycle but punish the actual work of triaging 80 requests on a Monday morning. The kanban view becomes an optional secondary mode (⌘ ⇧ B) for shift handoff, not the default.

Trade-off rejected: a five-column board reads as “ops product” and matches Front. The work pattern at 80 req/day doesn't tolerate the per-card real estate.

P2 · “AI must look like assistance, not authority” — agreed, but stronger

The brief asks for a subtle “AI draft” affordance. I'd go further: AI output is structurally separated from human-authored content, lives in its own surface region with a distinct background tint and a left border, and never collapses into a chat bubble that looks like a vet wrote it. One subtle pill is not enough when the output is a full reply draft about a dog's vomiting. The visual contract is: “a vet did not say this until they press ⏎.” See §06.

P3 · Three urgency levels are too few for triage realities

EU vet clinics have a stable triage pattern: routine, today, same-hour, life-threat. Your contract says low/medium/high. I'd map that to a 4-tier model in the UI (Routine · Today · Now · Critical) while keeping low|medium|high as the database enum, mapping high → "Now" and exposing Critical as a flag that always pages on-call. Reason: nurses already think in those four buckets. If we ship three, they'll abuse high as “needs to happen today” and lose signal for true emergencies.

If the data contract is locked, I'll fold critical into a separate risk_flag instead. Decision needed (Q-04).

P4 · Light mode v1, dark in v1.1 is correct, but

Reception desks at 7am and after-hours on-call shifts at 11pm are the highest-volume windows. Vets explicitly request dark mode within 4 weeks, every time. I'd ship dark mode in v1 alongside light, and use the time saved on... every other thing in the brief. Cost: ~5% extra design time, all token-driven. Benefit: stops the inevitable v1.1 fire drill.

P5 · The five-state Reminder model has a hole

Scheduled, Sent, Acknowledged, Completed, Missed. Missing: Snoozed (owner replied “tomorrow please”) and Cancelled (clinic cancelled appointment). Without these, missed-by-snooze pollutes the Missed bucket and skews KPIs. Add both, defer to product on whether they appear in the public KPI set.

01Principles

What we optimise for, in order.

  1. 1  ·  Density without noise

    Information per square inch is the headline metric. We strip chrome before we add it. Cards, drop shadows, gradients, container borders — default off. We rely on type weight, vertical rhythm, and a single hairline rule (1px --line) for separation.

  2. 2  ·  The inbox is the dashboard

    No KPI tile strip above the list. The work is the work. If clinic admins need numbers, they get a separate Reports surface, not a tile-sandwich on top of triage.

  3. 3  ·  Keyboard is primary, mouse is fallback

    Every primary action has a shortcut, shown on hover and in ⌘ K. The interaction grammar is vim-style: object-then-verb (select request → assign), navigation with j/k, action with single letter. See §08.

  4. 4  ·  Calm at the edges, urgent at the centre

    Owner surfaces (WhatsApp, web intake) read as warm, slow, reassuring. Staff surfaces read as cold, fast, exact. Same brand, two registers. We do not make staff UI “friendly” — vets read that as condescension, and consumer-pet-brand iconography is a hard no.

  5. 5  ·  AI is a second author, never the author

    Every AI artefact carries provenance: model, prompt version, confidence, and a visible Accept / Edit / Regenerate / Reject control. Nothing AI-generated reaches an owner without a human keystroke. Nothing AI-generated looks like it was written by the clinic. See §06.

  6. 6  ·  Trust is rendered, not claimed

    Audit trail is not a settings link — it is rendered inline in request detail (collapsed by default, one click to expand). “Last edited by Liis 14:02” is part of the type spec, not an afterthought.

  7. 7  ·  Localisation is the visual system, not a wrapper

    EN/ET/RU is foundational (localization.md). Estonian compound words run 30–40% longer than English. Russian Cyrillic shifts vertical metrics. Type scale, button paddings, table column widths are tested in all three locales before sign-off — not after.

  8. 8  ·  Reduced motion is the canonical motion

    We design the no-motion variant first. Animations are 150–200ms ease-out, layered on top, and never carry meaning that's lost without them. If a status change requires a slide animation to be understood, the status change is wrong.

02Brand

Visual register: a healthcare operations tool that respects its users.

References (in priority order): Linear for density and shortcut grammar; Stripe Dashboard for tabular clarity; Vercel for type discipline; Apple Health for the owner-side calm only. Anti-references: every consumer pet brand, every “modern” SaaS landing page in the last 18 months.

Wordmark & product chrome

PetCura wordmark is set in the body face, weight 500, no custom letterforms. The clinic identifier (multi-tenant) sits adjacent in the chrome — quiet, always visible, never shouting. Format: PetCura · Tartu Loomakliinik in the top-left, body weight 400. No logo lockup, no badge.

Type

I'd take Geist Sans over Inter. Geist's ink trap and tabular numbers are slightly better at small sizes (12–13px), and its cv11 alternate gives us the straight-sided l and single-storey a that read better in numeric-heavy tables (request IDs, timestamps, phone numbers). Three weights only: 400 body, 500 emphasis & UI labels, 600 headings only. Geist Mono for IDs, timestamps, kbd, code, and the audit log — this gives the audit trail an instantly-recognisable visual register.

If Geist's licensing is a friction (Vercel restrictions on commercial use in some regions), Inter is the no-risk fallback with identical token mapping.

Colour

The codebase ships a teal-on-cream palette and a body gradient. I'd keep the teal primary (#0e6a64, slightly desaturated from the existing #0f766e for fewer chroma fights with red urgency) and remove the body gradient entirely — it contradicts “operational density.” Background goes to #fbfbfa (warm off-white, neutral, distinct from --paper).

Semantic colour discipline:

  • Teal only for primary affordances and selected states. Not for “active link” in body text — ink stays ink.
  • Red only for Critical urgency and destructive confirmations. Never for validation errors on owner-facing forms (use ink + helper copy).
  • Amber only for Now / today urgency and AI low-confidence states.
  • Green only for resolved and delivered indicators. Never for “success toast” — toast is neutral with a green dot.

Iconography

Lucide, 1.5px stroke, 16×16 default. Filled variants are reserved for binary status indicators only (delivered ✓, read ✓✓, missed ●). No decorative icons in row labels. No paw prints, no animal silhouettes anywhere in staff UI. Owner intake may use one calm illustration on the success state — placeholder only until we commission.

Motion

Three motion tokens: --motion-fast 120ms, --motion 180ms, --motion-slow 240ms; all cubic-bezier(.2,.6,.2,1) (ease-out, no overshoot). One use only: state changes (open/close, in/out, list reorder). No page transitions. No spring physics. Reduced-motion swaps duration to 1ms globally — no opacity-only fallbacks, just instant.

04Information architecture

App shell, navigation, and the “view” model.

The staff app is a two-pane shell: collapsible left rail (icons + labels, 240px / 56px collapsed), main content area. There is no top bar — clinic identifier and user menu live at the foot of the left rail, where they belong (Linear pattern). This buys us 56px of vertical height on every screen, which is the most expensive real estate in the app.

Left rail — top to bottom

  • Inbox — the default landing surface, with sub-items for saved views (My open · Unassigned · Urgent · Today · Mentions · All).
  • Reminders — full reminder list view.
  • Reports — AI usage, response times, volume. Admin only.
  • Settings — clinic, integrations, members, audit log, AI usage cap.
  • — spacer —
  • Clinic identifier + user avatar at the foot.

The view model

Following Linear / Height: each item in the left rail under Inbox is a saved view — a stored filter + sort + grouping. Users add custom views with ⌘ ⇧ V. The five lifecycle states from the brief (New, Urgent, Waiting Staff, Waiting Owner, Resolved) become the default views, but the surface is a single dense list per view, not five columns. Kanban is a toggle (⌘ ⇧ B), not the default. See P1 push-back.

Owner surface IA

Single page, single column, no navigation. The owner is in WhatsApp 95% of the time; the web fallback at clinic.petcura.app/intake mirrors the WhatsApp flow as a vertical wizard with one question per screen on mobile, all-in-one on desktop. No tabs. No menu. There is one branch only: “Is this an emergency?” → either continue, or short-circuit to the clinic phone number with explicit copy.

05Surfaces

Four surfaces, with hierarchy intent and state plan.

A  ·  Owner intake (WhatsApp + web fallback)

Hierarchy. Owner sees one question at a time. We do not show progress bars (they imply effort); we show a quiet step indicator “Step 2 of 4” in muted grey. Each question has: question, helper copy, single input, primary action. Nothing else.

Locale-first. First screen is locale selection (EN / ET / RU) with the device default pre-selected. Subsequent copy mirrors WhatsApp tone: short, second-person, no jargon. “Mis on lemmiku nimi?”, not “Please enter your pet's name.”

States. default · loading (skeleton on the question card, not a spinner) · empty (irrelevant — never empty) · error (inline red helper text, no toast, no dialog).

Rationale. The emergency CTA is at the bottom and uses red because in a true emergency, owners should bail out of the form and call. The form converts against the call; we should not optimise it past safety.

B  ·  Clinic inbox

Hierarchy. Top: a single search/command bar (/ focuses). Below: a left rail of saved views. Right: a single dense list, one row per request, ~44px tall. Row reads as: status pill · urgency dot · pet name · owner name · category tag · last message preview · elapsed time · assignee avatar. Hover reveals quick actions on the right edge (assign, snooze, resolve). j/k moves selection. ⏎ opens detail.

Real-time presence. A subtle 4×4 dot in the assignee column when a colleague has the request open. Hover for the name. No avatars stacking, no “Liis is typing” spinner — that's noise.

States.

  • Default populated — as above. 25-50 rows visible at 1080p without scrolling at t/base.
  • Loading — 8 skeleton rows, no spinner. Skeletons match the row template.
  • Empty — “No open requests in this view.” + a single secondary action (“Show all statuses”). No mascot. No celebration. The empty state is information, not reward.
  • Error — inline banner above the list: “Couldn't load requests. Retry.” The list area dims, doesn't disappear — previous data stays usable.

C  ·  Request detail

Layout. Two-pane. Left pane (~58%): conversation thread with translation toggle, internal notes interleaved with timestamps but visually separated. Right pane (~42%): pet card, owner card, AI summary, status timeline, action bar pinned to bottom. The right pane is collapsible (⌘ ]) for full-width thread view.

Action bar. Bottom of left pane, sticky. Reply textarea + the seven actions from the brief, ordered by frequency: Reply primary · Insert AI draft · Assign · Set urgency · Create reminder · Mark resolved · overflow: Export PDF, Add note, Reopen.

Audit trail. Status timeline is rendered inline in the right pane, collapsed to last 3 events; expand reveals all. Each line: monospace timestamp + actor + verb + previous→new. This is the trust surface; it's not in settings.

Translation. Toggle is per-message and per-thread. Original text always retrievable (hover, or ⌥ hold). Translation is rendered inline, with a hairline rule and the source language label. AI translation cards carry the same provenance affordance as summaries.

D  ·  Reminders

List view. Same density grammar as the inbox: dense list, one row per reminder, status pill leading. Columns: state · pet · owner · title · scheduled at · channel · created by. n creates new.

Creation flow. From any request: r opens an inline composer at the bottom of the right pane (not a modal — modals interrupt). Fields: title, body (with locale per channel), schedule, channel (WhatsApp / SMS), repeat. AI suggests title and body, marked as draft. Save commits to scheduled.

State chips. Scheduled · Sent · Acknowledged · Completed · Missed (per brief) + Snoozed · Cancelled (per P5).

06AI assistance

The single most consequential pattern in this product.

Three rules: AI output is visually distinct, AI output is provenanced, AI output is never auto-sent. Every AI artefact in the staff UI is rendered in an AI card with these elements:

▲ AI summary model · v3 · conf 0.78 · 14:02 ──────────────────────────────────── Lameness, back-left, ~4h onset. Eating normal. Photo shows mild swelling. [ accept ] [ edit ] [ ↺ regenerate ] [ × reject ]
  • Marker. Left-edge accent (teal at conf ≥ 0.7, amber at 0.4–0.7, muted-grey below). The accent is the AI signature; no other element in the UI uses a left-edge accent.
  • Provenance line. Monospace, muted, 11px. Contains: model, prompt_version, confidence, generated_at. From ai-output-contracts.md. Click for full ai_logs entry.
  • Actions. Always four: accept, edit, regenerate, reject. Single-keystroke shortcuts within the card (a / e / g / r) once focused.
  • Diff on regenerate. When a user regenerates, the previous output is held in a side-by-side diff (added in green dot, removed in muted strikethrough). One-keystroke accept of either version.
  • Insertion. Accepting an AI reply draft moves the text into the staff Reply textarea — it does not send. The send action is a second deliberate keystroke, by a human.
  • Cost meter. Per ai-safety: a small monospace indicator in the right-pane footer shows AI usage this month as a percentage of cap. Three states: under 80% (muted), 80–100% (amber), over (red, with a non-blocking inline note in the AI card: “Cap reached. Regeneration disabled until reset.”).

What we explicitly will not do. No animated “sparkles.” No purple gradients. No streaming text in the staff UI for short outputs (it implies authority and slows the workflow); we render in one frame after a 600ms shimmer skeleton. Streaming is reserved for long reply drafts where the user actively waits.

07Components

Scope, approach, and state coverage.

Component library lives in packages/ui. Three primitives already exist (Button, Badge, Panel, Metric); we'll extend, not replace. Headless behaviour lifted from Radix; visuals are token-driven.

States covered for every component: default · hover · focus-visible · active · disabled · loading · error · selected · readonly. Where a state is not meaningful (Avatar has no “disabled”), we say so explicitly in the spec.

09Open questions

Answer these before I start v0.2 (high-fi).

Priority: blocking · shapes the system · can defer.

Q-01

Inbox layout: dense list (default) with kanban toggle, or true five-column kanban?

My recommendation in §00 P1 is list-first. Lock this before component-state work; the InboxRow vs ColumnCard primitives diverge significantly.

Q-02

Geist Sans — OK to commit, or fall back to Inter?

If there's any procurement / legal hesitation around Geist's licence terms, I'd rather know now than redraw 18 mocks.

Q-03

Dark mode in v1 or v1.1?

Per P4, I want v1. Costs ~5% extra design time. Decision impacts token spec sign-off.

Q-04

Three urgency tiers (per contract) or four in the UI (Routine / Today / Now / Critical)?

Per P3. If we keep three, “Critical” becomes a flag, not a tier — affects status pill, urgency selector, and pager integration.

Q-05

Translation toggle default: auto-translate to staff locale or show source, translate on demand?

Auto-translate is faster but loses original tone signal (worried owner vs polite owner reads differently). I lean source-first; you may have user research that says otherwise.

Q-06

Multi-tenant chrome: clinic name only, or clinic name + a small clinic-chosen accent colour?

A 4px clinic accent on the left rail helps disambiguate when staff serve multiple clinics. Risk: dilutes the system. Recommend the accent only when a user has access to ≥ 2 clinics.

Q-07

Real-time presence: viewer dots only, or also “X is replying” banner?

Banner prevents collisions but is the most distracting kind of motion. Recommend: dot only, with a non-modal toast at the moment of conflict (“Liis just sent a reply.”).

Q-08

AI usage cap behaviour at 100%: hard-stop regeneration, or soft-warn and continue?

Hard-stop respects metering, soft-warn respects clinical urgency. Recommend hard-stop with an admin-only override (⌘ ⇧ G) audited.

Q-09

Reminder additions: ship Snoozed and Cancelled in v1, or only Snoozed?

Snoozed is the higher-value add (owner-driven). Cancelled can be expressed as a completed with a reason in v1 if we want to defer.

Q-10

Owner-side branding: clinic logo allowed in the WhatsApp/web flow header?

Clinics will ask. I'd allow a single 1-line clinic name in a chosen weight; no logo upload in v1 to avoid an arms race over header design.

Q-11

PDF export visual treatment: identical to in-app, or print-optimised (no colour, monochrome timeline)?

Recommend: print-optimised. PDFs are forwarded to PMS; colour is wasted ink and can survey-print poorly.

Q-12

Variation breadth: one direction in high-fi, or two parallel directions (e.g. ultra-dense Linear-style vs slightly-warmer Stripe-style) for comparison?

If you want to validate the density bet against a less aggressive option, two directions is cheap on tokens but doubles screen-state work. Default to one unless you ask.
